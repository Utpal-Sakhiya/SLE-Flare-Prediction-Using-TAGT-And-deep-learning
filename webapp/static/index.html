What is an SLE flare?

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE, "lupus") is an autoimmune disease: the immune system attacks the body's own skin, joints, kidneys and blood. It moves between calmer spells and flares, periods when inflammation and symptoms get clearly worse.

Clinicians score disease activity with SLEDAI-2K, a checklist of clinical and lab findings where 0 means inactive and higher means more active. An early warning that a flare is likely could bring a check-up forward or prompt a treatment review.

What this system does

For one visit it takes — gene-expression values from a blood sample, the current SLEDAI-2K score and whether the patient is flaring now. It outputs a risk score for a flare at the next visit, plus the probes that moved that score most.

A clinical-only logistic regression (SLEDAI-2K + flaring now) runs beside TAGT as a baseline, and the Results page compares every model on patients it never saw in training.

How TAGT works, in three steps

  1. 1

    Gene encoder

    — standardised probe values pass through heavy input dropout and a two-layer network that compresses them to a 128-number summary.

  2. 2

    Projection + attention

    A projection layer and 8-head self-attention re-weight that gene summary.

  3. 3

    Clinical fusion

    SLEDAI-2K and flaring-now are encoded, joined to the gene summary, and a small classifier outputs the flare risk score.

    Methodology

    Where the data comes from, how the models were trained and validated, and what the evaluation cannot tell you.

    Dataset provenance

    Semi-synthetic. The expression profiles are real whole-blood microarray data from GEO series GSE49454 (Illumina). The patients, their visit sequences, SLEDAI-2K scores and flare labels are simulated (src/data/process_real_data.py); each simulated visit was given a randomly drawn real profile plus noise.

    Because profiles were attached to visits at random, expression carries little real link to the outcome; gene-based models can only find weak or spurious signal.

    Inputs and target

    • Gene expression: — log2 probe values, standardised per probe with the training fold's mean and SD.
    • SLEDAI-2K at the current visit (scaled by 1/20).
    • Flaring now: 0 or 1.
    • Target: a flare at the next visit.

    Architecture (deployed variant)

    • Gene encoder: input dropout 0.7 → 1,000→256, ReLU, dropout 0.3 → 256→128.
    • Projection 128→128 with ReLU, then 8-head self-attention.
    • Clinical encoder 2→32; classifier 160→128→64→1 with dropout 0.3.

    Here the "graph" step is a dense projection, not a gene–gene network, and attention sees one visit at a time, not a sequence of visits.

    Training

    • Optimiser AdamW, learning rate 1e-3, weight decay 0.05.
    • 15 epochs, batch size 32, gradient clipping at 1.0.
    • Input dropout 0.7 on the gene branch, plus Gaussian noise (σ 0.1) on the standardised expression.
    • Class-weighted binary cross-entropy (positive weight = non-flares ÷ flares).

    Validation

    —.

    • All visits from one patient stay in one fold, so every reported score comes from patients the model never saw.
    • AUC-ROC is computed on the pooled out-of-fold scores; fold AUCs are reported as mean ± SD.
    • Thresholds maximise Youden's J on those same out-of-fold scores, so accuracy, sensitivity and specificity are slightly optimistic.
    • The served models are then refit on every visit, so dataset-visit predictions on the Predict page are in-sample.

    Baselines

    • Logistic regression (clinical): SLEDAI-2K and flaring now only, standardised.
    • Logistic regression (genes + clinical): all 1,002 inputs, strong L2 penalty (C = 0.01).
    • Random forest (genes + clinical): 300 trees, at least 3 samples per leaf.

    Every model is trained and scored on the same folds.

    About the earlier 0.943 AUC

    Older documents in this repository report an AUC of 0.943. That pipeline fed the model the change in SLEDAI-2K from the current visit to the next one (next_sledai − current_sledai in src/training/train_breakthrough_v2.py), which is information from the visit being predicted, and it did not keep each patient's visits in a single fold.

    With that leaked feature removed and patient-grouped cross-validation, the result does not reproduce. The figures on the Results page replace it.

    Limitations

    • Semi-synthetic data. Visits and labels are simulated; nothing here is evidence of clinical performance.
    • Small cohort. — patients, so each test fold holds about — and fold AUCs swing widely.
    • Uncalibrated scores. A risk score ranks visits; it is not "the chance of a flare".
    • No external validation on an independent cohort.
    • Attributions are local sensitivities of one model, not biological findings.
